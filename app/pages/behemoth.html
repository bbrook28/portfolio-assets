My contribution

The first castle, and the last place.

Behemoth's levels are large and open, letting players approach objectives their own way between the giant boss fights. I worked on both ends of that journey: the concept for the final area, and a castle hub in the opening chapter that teaches players how these spaces work.

Opening chapter · Castle hub

One arch, three ways forward.

Players arrive through a large entryway that frames the castle and sets its scale. It opens onto an outdoor courtyard built around a central arch: a landmark visible from everywhere in the space, so players always know where they are.

From the courtyard, the hub connects to two other areas. It is an early lesson in the game's structure: a readable central space with multiple routes, rather than a single corridor.

  • Large entryway
  • Outdoor courtyard
  • Central arch (landmark)
  • Connections to two areas

In the shipped game

From blockout to snowbound ruin.

The finished courtyard, as built and lit by the environment art team. The gate entrance opens onto the courtyard, the arched bridge anchors the middle of the space, and routes lead off to the chapel ruins and the tower.

Finale · Area concept

Where the hunt ends.

I developed the concept for the game's final area, the setting for the climactic fight against the Hollow King. The space has to deliver on hours of buildup: the biggest sense of scale in the game, a clear read of the arena in VR, and a stage that lets the last behemoth feel like the payoff.
