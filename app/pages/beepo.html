Interactive · Polarity

Pull together, push apart.

Drag either robot, then flip the polarity. Attract closes the gap; repel throws them apart. The magnets only engage when the pair face each other within range, the same rule that made every gap and wall in the levels a question of positioning.

Making force visible

If players can't see it, they can't plan with it.

Magnetism is invisible, so my VFX and UI work was about readability: when the magnets are live, what direction the force is acting, and whether the pair is lined up close enough to engage.

Audio did the same job. The team's sound list ties each effect to the state it has to communicate.

Post-jam

What 48 hours missed.

Players liked the idea and the puzzles. They also found the edges of a physics puzzle built in a weekend, which is the most useful feedback a jam gives you.

  1. Teach the escape hatch

    The reset key existed, but players didn't know. Recovery tools belong in the tutorial, not the store page.

  2. Guard the goal

    One robot could reach the exit alone and leave the other stranded. Win states for co-op should check for both.

  3. Clamp the physics

    Magnet force plus collision produced clipping and softlocks. Limiting range and pushing objects out of walls would have closed most of them.
