Origin

From class project to commercial release.

Sumo Scuffle started as a four-person SCAD prototype that I designed and programmed. I then rebuilt it alone as a full commercial release: design, programming, art and audio direction, playtesting, App Store submission, and marketing.

The goal was a game anyone can pick up at a party. That meant short bouts, simple controls, and chaos that stays readable when four people are crowded around one phone.

  • ModesFeast, King of the Ring, Keep-Away, and Sumo Bowling
  • Couch-first2–4 players on one device, with a face-to-face layout for a phone lying flat on a table
  • Solo tooCPU opponents tuned by a hidden skill rating

Playtesting · Designing for non-gamers

Every playtest changed the controls.

I playtested with people who rarely play games, and each round reshaped how the game is controlled and taught.

  1. Tilt by default

    Testers fought the joystick, so I added tilt controls: tip the phone to roll, tap anywhere to charge. They're now the default.

  2. Flick to charge

    One tester held the phone in one hand and steered with a finger they never lifted, so they couldn't charge. I added flick-to-charge and an in-game tip that teaches it.

  3. Play first, choose later

    New players were asked to pick controls before they'd moved at all. Now the Dojo starts them rolling straight away, asks "Like these controls?" afterwards, and lets them switch schemes live.

  4. Tips that step aside

    Lesson tips covered what testers were trying to do, so they now shrink out of the way once you start moving.

  5. Reward the aim

    A tester mashed the charge button, so missed charges now recharge more slowly.

Modes · Readable chaos

Four ways to shove.

Four players and falling food can turn into noise fast. Every system is there to keep the bout readable and the score close.

  • Power-upsRead at a glance: a split-second slow-down, a light pillar and shockwaves on pickup, and a colored ring under your sumo that blinks before it wears off
  • RicochetsPinball-style drums bounce wrestlers around the ring
  • Catch-upHelp for whoever is behind, plus a double-points final rush
  • ReplayAn automatic slow-motion replay of each bout's best moment
  • SurprisesMid-bout events that shake up a match

Systems design

Difficulty without a menu.

A hidden skill rating tunes the CPU opponents after every bout, so nobody can pick Easy and coast.
