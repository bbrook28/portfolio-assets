Enemy design · Eradicators

Mama's elite guard.

The Tower's strongest troops, saved for fights with a heavy Tower presence. The goal was a new threat for players who had mastered Chapter 1 and were carrying advanced weaponry.

Every rule pushes the player toward a different answer than a headshot:

  • ArmorHelmets and face coverings make headshots unreliable
  • CounterOne grenade-launcher round kills a human, two kill a walker
  • BehaviorRushes the player and only takes cover when injured
  • LoadoutsFaster-firing M4s, more accurate Supernova shotguns
  • CostBuilt on the existing enemy system to stay cheap to produce

Enemy design · Grenadiers

Get the player moving.

VR players love to hunker behind cover. Grenadiers lob explosives to flush them out, carry .30 revolvers for close quarters, and give the Tower AI a way to deal with walker hordes, including Eradicator walkers.

  • Range550–2,000 units, tunable per spawn
  • Fuse6 s, plus 2.5 s if the player catches it
  • SafetyWon't throw near allies
  • Data-drivenGrenade type, arc height and fuse set in Blueprint
VR detail: catching a live grenade adds time to its fuse, which gives players a beat to throw it back.

Weapon design · New arsenal

Every new weapon, from concept to ship.

I designed, concepted, tested and implemented all of Retribution's new weapons and attachments, and rebalanced returning guns, working alongside the art, audio and animation teams to make each one feel distinct in the hand.

  • ChainsawCuts through anything in its path, balanced by its weight and its need for fuel
  • Sawed-off shotgunClose-range horde control, small enough to dual-wield
  • SMGFast, sustained fire for crowds
  • Grenade launcherHeavy explosive power, and the Eradicators' counter
  • SilencersQuieter shots, so firearms don't always pull the horde
  • Laser sightsFit almost any firearm for steadier aim and headshots
  • RebalancedThe returning assault rifle and 9mm pistol, retuned to sit alongside the new arsenal
My part: concept, stats and tuning, recoil and reload feel, haptics, crafting recipes, and implementation in Blueprints, iterated through playtests with art, audio and animation. You can see the new weapons in action in the gameplay video at the end of this page.

Interactive · Chainsaw haptics

Making a chainsaw feel like a chainsaw.

I designed and tuned every haptic curve on the chainsaw, one of the most praised interactions in Retribution. The goal was for players to hear, see and feel each state, from the first pull of the ripcord to the sputter of an empty tank, with meat and bone cutting differently.

These are the shipped intensity curves. Pick one to see its shape and watch the controller react.

0 s

On a phone? Android plays the full vibration pattern. iPhone gives one haptic tick per tap.

Playtest feedback

Ripcord in three beats

Feedback asked for a slight buzz on the initial pull, moderate at half pull and intense at full pull, so the start-up builds like a real engine.

Hardware

One curve isn't one feel

Haptic strength differs between PS VR2, Quest and other headsets, so curves were checked per platform. PS VR2's headset motor and adaptive triggers opened up more options.

Bug hunting

The right hand

Testing caught the ripcord haptic firing on the wrong hand and overlapping SFX. Both hands now feel the saw while it runs.

Mission design · Tower Mission 2

Teaching a system by using it.

The original mission relied on bespoke scripting. I rebuilt it around the game's real Exile Order system, so the mission doubles as the tutorial for a feature players use for the rest of the game.

Patricia, an Exile trader on Bourbon Street, asks for surgical scissors from the dangerous clinic, everyday loot from other maps, and UV mushrooms that only glow at night. One order teaches the clinic threat, the Exile economy and Night Mode's UV loot.

  1. Meet Patricia at night

    Travel to Bourbon Street and meet the Exile Trade Network leader.

  2. Take a real Exile Order

    5 surgical scissors, 3 generic loot, 4 UV night loot.

  3. Open-world gathering

    The clinic is playable day or night, with Gerik's threat looming the whole time.

  4. Close the loop

    Complete the order and return the bolt cutters to the dissidents.

Systems design · Night Mode

Dead men's backpacks.

A loot layer only for Night Mode. Fresh corpses of survivors who died during the day spawn with backpacks of night-only items, so players who brave the dark find loot in a more personal, dynamic way.

I wrote the spec as a toolset for designers, not a fixed table, so the system could be tuned without code changes.

  • SpawningDesigner-set min/max bodies per map, with a weighted distribution
  • Loot tiersSeparate "Average" and "Rare" find tables, each weighted
  • GuaranteesMinimum number of rare bodies per visit
  • InteractionBackpacks open with the same UX as the player's own
  • RulesNo backpacks inside backpacks; pages generated from contents
  • StretchTurn a pack upside down and shake it to empty it

Level design · Exile camps

Every Exile camp in New Orleans.

I designed and implemented all of the Exile camps across the game's maps, from scouting each site to the finished, playable camp. Each one is a defensible pocket in an existing space: a lumberyard, a garage beside a shipping office, a coach's office in a school gym, a covered parking area at the mansion, a reception floor in the hotel. Top-down passes mark the footprint, and in-engine shots check how it reads on the ground.

Economy

Survival tension

Placed resources and tuned crafting costs and ammo economy to keep players on the edge between scarcity and power.

Layouts

Non-linear missions

Designed open mission spaces with stealth and combat approaches and vertical routes that reward VR climbing.
