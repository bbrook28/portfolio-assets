What I owned
The central hub: the courthouse, the police station and the tunnel between them, from paper maps to a playable Unreal Engine 5 slice.
The brief

"Treat the courthouse like a dungeon"

The problem

The narrative team's brief gave the lobby, courtroom and offices each a piece of the town's story. The layout had to support stealth, combat and exploration in VR.

What I did

I took the hub from top-down paper maps to a playable slice, with more than a dozen ways into the courthouse and locks that reopen wings players already passed.

The result

The slice was finished and fully playable. The studio then canceled the project to focus on other IP.

Swiss cheese

Sewers, roof, windows and broken walls: more than a dozen ways in, so stealthy and aggressive players each find an approach.

Locks you can see

Broken stairwells, flooded basements and barricaded doors block the way. Keys and upgrades reopen wings players already passed.

One world

The tunnel joins a civic courthouse and an industrial police compound with no break in the walk.

Sized for VR

The jail cells are 10 by 10, the jury room got smaller, and the tunnel fits two people, with a 45° turn that blocks sightlines.

Paper to path

Room layout to player route

Every space started as a top-down paper map. The overview fixes rooms and doors. The gameplay pass draws the route, red X's for blocked paths, enemies, loot and hidden crawl-throughs. Drag to compare.
