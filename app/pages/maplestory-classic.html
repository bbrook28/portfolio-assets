Level design

Rules for every map

New maps in an old game get judged against the old maps. I measured Nexon's original Victoria Island maps and wrote the numbers down as rules. When a rule and the original maps disagree, the original maps win.

  1. SpawnsWeak to strong from the entrance

    Players walk in past the easiest monsters, and monster levels only go up as the maps go deeper.

  2. CampsCamps stay clear

    No spawns on the floors under Jaime's, Daniel's and Tamya's camps, so players can turn in quests in peace.

  3. PlatformsBig monsters need big platforms

    Myewoods and Sporewoods only spawn on platforms at least 350 px wide, about the smallest the original maps use.

  4. CrowdingCrowding is measured

    I count the monsters alive at once from map size and spawn rate, the way the map editor does, and compare each floor with the originals.

  5. QuestsQuest monsters keep their numbers

    A spawn can move to another map, but the total a quest depends on stays the same.

  6. QAEvery change gets a before and after

    Each map edit ships with rendered before and after images, so QA can see what moved.

Before and after · Decayed Tunnel III

Moving a Myewood off a ledge

An audit found a Myewood on a 283 px ledge, too small for it. I moved the spawn to a full floor. In another pass the bottom floor was 75% crowded, so I moved three Myewood spawns next door to Lingering Regret. That brought it to 47% and kept the Myewood total Myra's quest needs.

Encounter design

The field boss

Field boss · Lv 60

Rotten Mushmom

  • An undead Mushmom, 20 levels above the Victoria Island original.
  • She only spawns in Someone Else's Grave, a hidden map off Decayed Tunnel I. Players find her by exploring, or through Glimmer's questline.
  • She regenerates and is weak to holy and fire, the same classes the rest of the Deep Passage favors. That makes her a party fight.
Tools and docs

Tools I wrote for the team

The game's data is edited by hand, and mistakes show up in the client, not the editor. I wrote four Python tools to catch them earlier, shared them with the team and documented them.

MapView

Draws any map from the game data, clean or with footholds, portals and spawns. It flags layout errors, measures crowding floor by floor and renders a before and after of each change. Every map image on this page came from it.

NPC Hi-Res

Exports NPC, monster and item sprites at four times their size, as PNGs, layered PSDs and pose sheets, plus a reference pack for outside artists. I used it for the art requests on new NPCs and decor.

Quest Tool

Compares each quest's written spec on the wiki with the quest data and lists every place they disagree. Its tester runs test plans through quests and party quests in a local client.

Dev Tool

One window that sets up and starts the local servers and test client for a playtest.
