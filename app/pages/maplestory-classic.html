The role

A new place in a world players already know by heart.

I'm a Game Designer on MapleStory Classic World, a live-service 2D side-scrolling MMORPG (an online role-playing game played by many players at once) that rebuilds classic-era MapleStory. I joined in June 2026 and worked through Founder's Access on October 6 and Grand Launch on October 21, on Steam and the Nexon launcher, and into live service.

The work is hands-on, from design spec to shipped data. I build quests, NPCs, maps, items, and rewards directly in the game's data files, then take them through QA (quality assurance testing) to release: about 300 commits in five months.

My lead project is Forgotten Hollow, a brand-new region for levels 39–59 that never existed in the original game. It had to feel like it had always been there, hidden under Ellinia.

What I built in Forgotten Hollow

  • QuestsDozens of quests across several story chains: dialogue, objectives, kill and collect counts, level gates, hand-offs between quest givers, and rewards, including gear every class can use.
  • MapsBuilt and reworked most of the region's maps: layout, platforming, ropes and ladders, collision, portals, camera bounds, backgrounds, and minimaps. Several were redesigned after playtests.
  • Monster placementPlacement and density: safe camps around quest givers, the strongest monsters far from entrances, crowding balanced against map size. I wrote a rulebook so map audits stay consistent.
  • NPCs and itemsNew NPCs with dialogue and idle chatter, quest items with icons and drop sources, and themed housing decor sold in a shop, crafted by players, or given as quest rewards.
  • Release planningGated for a staged rollout, so the whole region opens on update day.
  • QualityFull review passes on every quest (text, rewards, EXP, prerequisites) and every map (structure, spawns, crowding) before handing off to QA.

Environmental storytelling

The deeper you go, the sicker it gets.

The Hollow's story is about a rot spreading through the World Tree's roots, so the theming carries it before any dialogue does. Near the surface the forest is green, open, and lit. By the Deep Passage the palette has turned to bruised purples and rusted reds.

Monsters follow the same arc. Glowshrooms and Aqumanders up top give way to undead Rotten Mushrooms and the regenerating Sporewood at the bottom, so level, difficulty, and dread all rise together.

Quest architecture · Quest log

One way in, three ways forward, one way home.

The story opens in Ellinia's Magic Library and closes there too. A short linear act pulls players into the Hollow, then opens three parallel chains gated by level instead of order, so players progress as they grow without waiting on one long chain.

01

Bookended in the old world

Starting and ending with Grendel in the Magic Library stitches a brand-new region into a town players already love, and sets up what comes next.

02

Gates by level, not by order

Once players are inside, three chains open at once and unlock at 39, 45, and 49. Nobody is stuck behind a single bottleneck quest.

03

Quests that teach the map

Objectives send players to specific maps for specific monsters, so finishing the questlines naturally tours them through the whole Hollow.

No quests match that search.

Encounter design

An ecology that rewards every class.

Ten new monsters climb from level 39 to 59, each with a clear elemental identity. Fire, ice, lightning, and holy weaknesses are spread across the region, so Fire/Poison and Ice/Lightning mages, and Clerics against the undead, each have maps where they shine.

Spawns are mixed with returning Victoria Island monsters, like Zombie Mushrooms, Lupins, and Curse Eyes, so the Hollow feels connected to the world above.

Field boss · Lv 60

Rotten Mushmom

An undead take on a Victoria Island icon, 20 levels above the original. She only spawns in Someone Else's Grave, a hidden map off Decayed Tunnel I. Players find her by exploring, not from a quest marker. She regenerates health and is weak to holy and fire, which makes her a party fight built around the classes the rest of the Deep Passage favors.

Live now · Released October 2026

The Caretaker's Ledger

An original, multi-part story questline set around Ellinia's Magic Library, live in the game now. Lyn the Librarian is missing pages from an ancient registry, and recovering them sends players across Victoria Island to collect signatures from the leaders of each town.

Alongside the quests, I built a new hidden map, the Circulation Desk, reached through a secret portal and added to the world map; three new NPCs, Lyn the Librarian, Sabrina the Scribe, and Jon the Justicar, with dialogue and new sprites; and new quest items. The questline ends with the Faded Maple Leaf Earrings as a keepsake.

Why it matters: the chain ends with Grendel the Really Old, the same NPC who opens Forgotten Hollow. Players meet him through the Ledger before he needs them for the Hollow.

Live work · Already released

Shipping a launch, then keeping it running.

Beyond Forgotten Hollow, I worked across the live game before and after both launches.

Launch feature

Citizenship questlines

Dozens of quests in which players earn a home in Henesys and Kerning City, sending them across Victoria Island to hunt monsters, deliver items, and meet NPCs. Citizenship leads into Housing. I set the EXP, mesos (the in-game currency), item rewards, and level requirements.

Balance

Progression rebalance

Before launch, I retuned quest EXP and rewards across Maple Island and Victoria Island (Lith Harbor, Henesys, Ellinia, Kerning City, Perion, Sleepywood, and Florina Beach) to follow a new EXP curve, the pace at which players level up.

Launch support

Bug fixes and polish

Built quest data for the Stumpy and Mano field-boss quests (bosses that appear out in the open world) and fixed the Physical Fitness Challenge event map. Fixed collision bugs, including a 1-pixel gap that let mages teleport out of a map, ropes that grabbed players by accident, and broken footholds (the platforms characters stand on). Fixed quest bugs, including a reward that could be duplicated by re-accepting a quest, gear given to the wrong class, and missing dialogue. Edited hundreds of quest, NPC, item, and pet strings, and handled typos players reported after launch.

Approach

How I work.

  1. The design doc is the source of truth

    Data has to match it, and I keep the docs in sync with the data.

  2. Check every part of a change

    Text, hand-offs, prerequisites, level and EXP, rewards, and docs.

  3. Clear handoffs to QA

    I write clear handoffs to QA for every change.

  4. Conventional structure, creative content

    I follow the existing conventions in how data is structured and stay creative in the content players see.
