The problem

In a fight, she hands out ammo

Elizabeth is BioShock Infinite's emotional core, yet in combat she mostly throws the player ammo and health. Both pitches go after that gap between story and play, from two directions.

Pitch 01

The Tether Initiative

A system that ties Elizabeth's emotional state to the player's tactical choices.

Tether gauge

It fills when the two of them act together and unlocks Anchor abilities and duo finishers. When it's low, her support is limited.

Active partner

She calls out flankers, suppresses enemies with tear-based debris and reacts when the player takes damage.

Reads the room

She whispers and stays low in stealth and shouts targets in chaos, so her behavior matches the pacing.

Skill over luck

A predictable economy replaces the random supply toss, and tears become tools she moves on command.

Pitch 02 · Design test

A Vigor she draws on

Elizabeth sketches and paints all through Monument Tower. This Vigor turns that into play: Booker fires paper, Elizabeth draws on it, and what she draws becomes real.

Paper Cut

The light attack, a burst of paper that knocks enemies back and makes them bleed.

Paper Trail

The heavy attack lays a scroll on the ground, and Elizabeth draws a trap on it.

Context

Her traps match the room: Tesla coils in American history, spike pits in the Hall of Heroes.

Traversal

She can draw doorways and holes into marked walls and floors.

Character

Trap books unlock new drawings, and her aversion to killing can show up as non-lethal traps.

Level design document

One room that teaches it

A vandalized Columbia art gallery, drawn to scale on a one-foot grid. Each beat introduces one part of the Vigor, and once the player has it, beats 4 to 6 can happen in any order.

  1. Enter the gallery

    Elizabeth leads Booker toward a painting of Comstock.

  2. The defaced founder

    The Vox Populi painted an X over Comstock's face. She secretly adds to it when Booker isn't looking.

  3. The Vigor

    Light from the curved window falls on a display case. She picks the lock and Booker drinks.

  4. Easels

    Practice Paper Cut. Each easel holds a tear that plays sounds of the place it shows.

  5. The trap book

    She recognizes a hunting book from her library and draws a bear trap on the scroll.

  6. A draft from below

    She draws a hole in the floor and reveals a Gear.

  7. The locked door

    A scroll on the door becomes a lock she can pick, and they leave.
