Four rounds of the table

Before the poker hand, Sinners' Gambit was a race to 1,000 against up to six rivals, like the board game. I blocked out the table in flat shapes and a five-color palette, with only the stained glass and the dice as art, and reviewed each round at three phone sizes with four and seven players.

  1. Round 1The manuscript table

    The score track runs down the margin like notes in a manuscript.

  2. Round 2A wide track

    The track moves across the top, the candles get large and the active window doubles in size.

  3. Round 3True scores

    Each player's sigil sits at their true score on a full-width band, and the risk shows in your candle flames.

  4. Round 4Playable

    Small markers on stems, points over the dice and costs shown on what they hit. This round was the first playable table.

One hand

From first throw to payout

The current build is solo dice poker against the Master of the Dance. The guided first hand explains each rule on the screen where it first comes up, so nobody reads a rulebook before the first throw.

  1. KeepOne die a throw

    Tap a die to see the hand it makes and what it pays, then keep it.

  2. DrawChoose the risk

    Draw one, two or three dice. Each seal says how likely that draw is to lose the hand.

  3. SkullA 1 is a skull

    It stays out of the bag and snuffs a candle. Three skulls and the Master takes the hand.

  4. RowFive slots

    The rest of each throw goes back into the bag, so the odds change as the row fills.

  5. PayoutA full row is paid

    There is no cashing out early. Four of a kind pays 7 marks.

The rules

Why poker

Version 0.8.0 had a bag hand with its own scoring, and players had to learn a pile of rules before the first choice made sense. After playing it I wanted the game as simple as poker, so I rebuilt the hand around hands every player already knows.

Draw and keep

Each throw, draw one, two or three dice from the bag of seven and keep one in your row. The rest go back.

Skulls

A 1 stays out and snuffs one of the Master's three candles. The third skull loses the hand.

The last die

The bag's last die is thrown alone. It is wild, but a skull loses the hand.

Pay

Pair 1, two pair 2, three of a kind 3, straight and full house 5, four of a kind 7, five of a kind 12.

Prototype

The Sin's demand

After playing 0.11 I found the hand never asked a new question, and a third of keeps were coin flips. The next idea: each hand's Sin pays marks for something the poker hand punishes, so you spoil your hand a little to be paid. I priced every version with an exact solver before building it.

  1. No SinThe baseline

    Best play earns 3.58 marks a hand and busts 20% of the time.

  2. A lone 6 pays 4Don't pair your 6

    A second 6 and the Sin pays nothing. It changes the best keep in 26% of hands and busts drop to 17%.

  3. Held once pays 1Don't pair anything

    Each face you hold exactly once pays 1. It changes the best keep in 40% of hands.

The first demand, each 6 pays 2, failed the same test. A player who played poker and used the Sin only to break ties came within 2% to 5% of best play, so the Sin added no real choices.

Where it started

The board game

Sinners' Gambit began in 2016 as a tabletop game I made with Theresa Devine. I redesigned it on my own in 2025 and published it on The Game Crafter.

A race to 1,000

Players score 40, 50 or 60 for each 4, 5 or 6 they roll, and the first to 1,000 points escapes.

Purgatory

Roll nothing above a 3 and you go to purgatory. You climb out by rolling two or more dice from 1 to 3.

The Gambit Phase

Between rolls, players negotiate, trade, buy dice for 100 points and use their Sin's powers.

Seven Sins

Each Sin has three powers: one free, one that unlocks at 350 points and one you use once at 1,000.
